Origin: Fusing Five Disconnected Open Datasets & First Cloud Hardware Ray-Tracing Bring-Up

The project began from a fundamental data gap: high-resolution 3D terrain scans and competitive WRC rally telemetry are never published together. To build a physically grounded 3D Digital Twin for Toyota GAZOO Racing (TGR-WRT) without proprietary stage packs, we engineered an automated data-fusion pipeline combining five open scientific and government sources:

  • WRC 10 Hz Merged Telemetry & CUDA Optimal Ghost (#69 Rovanperä, #33 Evans, #00 Optimal): Synchronized speed, RPM, gear, throttle, brake, steering and 3D GPS traces across SS2/SS5 Elva, SS3/SS6 Neeruti, SS4/SS7 Otepää and Ruuhimäki, paired with an offline GPU forward-backward friction-circle optimal-control solver (#00).
  • University of Tartu Autonomous Driving Lab (UT-ADL) 30 Hz RTK-GNSS: Extracted厘米-accurate trajectories from summer2021-positions.parquet after discovering the exact local-to-L-EST97 coordinate offset (E = 650000 + x, N = 6465000 + y in EPSG:3301).
  • Estonian Land Board (Maa-amet) 1 m Float32 LiDAR DTM & Riegl VQ-1560i LAZ Point Clouds: Parsed raw 1 m elevation rasters and 2023 airborne laser scanning returns (ground class 2, water class 9, buildings class 6, vegetation classes 3/4/5) alongside 2048×2048 WMS aerial orthophotos.

Because the local workstation environment exposed only a virtual Microsoft Hyper-V Video adapter, we provisioned cloud GPU instances (vmCopilotA100 and vmCopilotV710). On the AMD Radeon Pro V710, the Linux kernel initially failed to initialize the GPU with missing firmware amdgpu/gc_11_0_3_mes1.bin (error -2). Injecting the gc_11_0_3 firmware suite into initramfs unlocked all 54 RDNA 3 hardware Ray Accelerators (VK_KHR_acceleration_structure + VK_KHR_ray_query), powering a custom C++/Vulkan ray-query bridge (libv710_vk_rt.so) over a 36,000-triangle terrain/car BLAS. Visually, however, the first live frames were purely geometric: trees were procedural green cones and the road was a simple ribbon.

The Grass & Micro-Surface Revolution: 260,000 Cubic-Bezier Hair Strands & 32-Bit Spline Interpolation

“I don't like the grass, this is just tufts here and there. We should have something like hair systems… Which rendering engine are we using actually? Should we switch to unreal or unity?”

Rather than scattering isolated cross-quad grass tufts (`v710_chase_moss_v1.jpg`), we architected a UE5-Groom / Ghost of Tsushima style instanced 3D curved Cubic-Bezier hair-ribbon grass system (while also generating a companion UE5.4 C++/Python project scaffold with Niagara dust and Chaos Vehicle configs):

  • 260,000 Dynamic 7-Vertex Curved Ribbons (1.82M vertices): Continuously recycled inside a camera-locked 92 m rolling field around the car, with species zones (tall golden-headed Calamagrostis reed grass on outer banks, lush Festuca meadow fescue on ditch slopes, and short Poa pratensis verge turf near the gravel edge).
  • Physical Fiber Shading: Combined Kajiya-Kay anisotropic longitudinal strand highlights with Marschner backlit subsurface chlorophyll transmission and a 6-layer volumetric moss shell raymarcher on ditch shoulders.
  • The 5.5 m Texture-Filter Snapping Bug: Early hair-grass renders exhibited periodic step-snapping along the road every 5.5 m. Root cause: ModernGL mapped a 2-tuple filter on a 32-bit float road-spline texture to (LINEAR, NEAREST), disabling hardware linear interpolation! Switching to explicit sub-texel texelFetch linear interpolation in GLSL eliminated the stepping completely. We also added 54,000 instanced 3D gravel pebbles in a 28 m rolling window.

Ground-Truth Rally Estonia Calibration, Depth-Buffer Shadow Fix & The Procedural Tree Dead-End

“ok stop, this is a dead end. do extensive research first to see best practices in generating procedural trees, failing that, just download very high poly tree models that fit… abandon these generated trees, they look terrible.”

With the grass carpet working, we audited the renderer against eight real WRC Rally Estonia photographs from Otepää, Elva and Tartu, upgraded the live stream pipeline from 720p to native 1920×1080 (bbb12a9), and uncovered two critical rendering bugs:

  • Missing Real 3D Shadows after Frame 0: Calling glDisable(GL_DEPTH_TEST) at the end of render_frame left depth testing disabled when the next frame's shadow_fbo pass began. Because OpenGL suppresses depth-buffer writes whenever GL_DEPTH_TEST is disabled, the 2048×2048 shadow map stayed frozen at 1.0 after frame 0, leaving only a rectangular analytic traceCarBox proxy under the car!
  • Double-Attenuated Ackermann Steering (0.075 × 0.075): Steering wheel angle was multiplied by 0.075 both during telemetry ingestion and inside render_frame, reducing a 25° wheel lock to an imperceptible 0.3°.
  • The Procedural Tree Card-Atlas Dead-End: We built a 5-column procedural tree atlas (baltic_3d_tree_atlas_v6) with normal-mapped bark and foliage cards (`v710_v6_low_orbit_trees.jpg`). Under close orbit inspection, intersecting alpha cards still read as flat paper cutouts. Following the user's pivot, we abandoned procedural card trees entirely in favor of real 3D botanical geometry.

From “Bare Brown Sticks” to the 4.10M-Triangle 8-Species Botanical Bundle

When we first imported raw 3D tree GLTF models from Poly Haven (`v710_ph3d_chase.jpg` and `v710_ph3d_forest_orbit.jpg`), the result was a visual shock: the trees rendered as dead, skeletal brown sticks. A four-part forensic diagnosis revealed why naive 3D plant imports fail at rally viewing distances:

  1. 97.5% Canopy Surface-Area Loss from Uncompensated Subsampling: The source conifer scans modeled 4.14M individual 1 cm × 2 mm 3D needles (148 m² total needle area). Subsampling to 100k triangles without scaling up the surviving needle clusters reduced canopy area to 3.6 m²!
  2. 75% Alpha-Card Knockout & Black Mip-Bleeding on 3D Needles: A twig-card alpha texture (75% transparent [0,0,0,0] pixels) was bound to already-3D-modeled individual needles and leaves. Worse, trilinear mipmapping averaged the transparent [0,0,0,0] background into the needle RGB texels (`mean_rgb = [27, 27, 15]`), turning surviving foliage pitch-black at distance.
  3. Dry South African Scan Albedo: The raw scans had drought-brown foliage (R > G, [101, 98, 46]) instead of lush July Estonian chlorophyll (G/R = 1.30).
  4. Missing Lower Canopy Skirts: High-branching scans had no foliage below y = 0.45, leaving an empty trunk hallway at the 2.8 m chase-camera eye height.

We built scripts/compile_ultra_highpoly_forest.py to compile 13 Poly Haven botanical scans into an 8-species × 3-LOD runtime bundle (4.10M LOD0 triangles, 2,068,548 vertices): finding exact topological needle/leaf clusters via scipy.sparse.csgraph.connected_components and scaling each cluster around its 3D centroid, forcing alpha = 255 with 5-pass morphological solid-color dilation, re-grading foliage to Estonian summer chlorophyll, and grafting lower canopy skirts (`uhp` v1 → v3 → v7).

12-Pass 3D Rotational Wheel Blur, Tire Contact Squish & Enclosed Kevlar Wheel Liners

In earlier builds, high-speed motion blur was approximated in the post-process shader using a hardcoded 120 px screen-space UV ellipse (carEllip, RCA-1), while the 3D wheel geometry itself was drawn in a single static pass (0 rotational shutter samples). At 142 km/h on SS2/SS5 Elva, this produced two simultaneous visual contradictions:

  • Smudged Bodywork + Frozen Spokes: The screen-space UV ellipse smeared the stationary Panasonic rear wing, #69 door number, and TOYOTA tailgate horizontally across 120 pixels, yet the white 12-spoke OZ Racing gravel rims remained completely frozen as if parked.
  • Hollow Wheel-Arch Sky Leaks (RCA-4) & Rigid Tires (RCA-3): Because the raw car shell lacked inner fender wells, bright sky and background grass leaked straight through the gap behind the front tires, and the tires floated rigidly without radial load deflection at the gravel contact patch.

The Fix: We removed carEllip screen-space smearing from the car body and implemented true 12-pass 3D rotational shutter accumulation (N_WHEEL_BLUR = 12 sub-frame rotor steps distributed across a 180° shutter angle Δθ = ω · Δtshutter around each wheel’s 3D hub axis), added radial contact-patch sidewall bulge (RCA-3), and sealed all four wheel wells with 3D matte Kevlar inner fender liners (RCA-4).

Articulated 5-Part Wheels: Topological Mesh Separation, Ackermann Steering & Braking Dive Kinematics

The raw Toyota GR Yaris Rally1 asset was delivered as a single monolithic static mesh — the wheels, tires, brake calipers, coilover struts, and chassis were all fused into one vertex buffer. To make the car respond physically to 10 Hz / 30 Hz WRC telemetry, we engineered a three-stage mechanical articulation pipeline:

  1. 5-Part Topological Connected-Component Separation: Using scipy.sparse.csgraph.connected_components combined with axle spatial bounding volumes (z_front = +1.23 m, z_rear = -1.32 m, y_axle = 0.33 m, r_wheel = 0.35 m), we partitioned the mesh into five independent articulated groups (body + FL, FR, RL, RR wheel/tire/rim assemblies) while keeping the upper orange/gold coilover suspension struts rigidly attached to the body shell so they never spin with the wheel (`yaris_5part_topological_split.jpg`).
  2. True Ackermann Kingpin Steering & Cumulative Axle Spin (ab66786): Early builds multiplied steering wheel telemetry by 0.075 during ingestion and again by 0.075 inside render_frame (0.075 × 0.075 = 0.005625), clamping a 25° cornering lock down to an invisible 0.3°! Fixing the double attenuation restored full ±25° kingpin yaw articulation (Y axis) composed with cumulative angular wheel integration θ(t) = ∫(v / rtire) dt around each hub’s local X axis (`zoom_articulated_wheels_macro.jpg`).
  3. Dynamic Braking Pitch, 880 °C Glowing Brake Rotors & Steered Rotor Blur (69d7330, 13cfd20): Under heavy braking entering the chicane, longitudinal deceleration drives forward chassis pitch/dive, compresses the front wheel arch down over the tire, heats the ventilated steel brake discs to a visible 880 °C orange-red thermal glow behind the calipers, and composes kingpin steering lock with 12-pass 3D rotational spoke blur (`zoom_braking_dive_macro.jpg`).

Act II · Chapter 04 · Atmosphere, Forensics & Cloud GPU Scale-Up (Commits a2aacbf → 63cbb26)

Nordic Golden Hour, the 15-still forensic audit, hydrodynamic water spray, and open sky-fill shadows.

As we migrated from the original V710 VM through NVIDIA A100 onto NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (48 GB vGPU), we stopped evaluating the renderer from a single flattering chase camera. Instead, we captured a 15-still forensic matrix covering pack chases, rural buildings, roadside props, dry gravel roost, puddle splashes, and forest interiors—and launched six parallel engineering tracks (RCA-1..8) to fix every defect we found:

  • Corridor-Relative Nordic Golden Hour & Open Sky-Fill Shadows (a2aacbf, 63cbb26): A fixed world-space sun vector pointed from behind the camera on north-west stages, killing forward scattering. Switching to a corridor-relative low sun (54° cross-track azimuth, 13.6° elevation, 3800K amber key) restored golden rim lighting. When full 3D tree shadow maps later stacked with ACES to crush forest interiors to ink-black (0.6% albedo), we decoupled Baltic blue sky-dome irradiance and warm ground bounce from direct sun occlusion.
  • UE5 Landmass-Style Graded Building Pads (RCA-5): Because terrain relief uses a 1.8× vertical presentation scale, a 14 m barn on a 12° hillside suffered a 5.3 m height delta across its footprint—burying its uphill wall to the roof eaves. Act IV · Chapter 07 · Closing the Micro-Surface & Water Realism Gap (Visualizer Current State)

32-step water reflections & Snell refraction, 480,000 true 3D gravel stones, and a 4.8-million-strand fur grass groom.

Once the macro geography and 80 Hz vehicle physics were in place, we began assembling our final sizzle movie in the Visualizer current state workstream. Immediately, close-up lead-car cameras and low-altitude helicopter passes exposed three remaining realism gaps on the ground and water—and drove three major shader and geometry breakthroughs:

  1. Why wasn’t the water reflecting or refracting? Inspection of v710_renderer.py revealed two culprits: water_vao was drawn immediately after terrain_vao (before 3D trees, buildings, grass, sky, and cars were in the framebuffer!), and at a 54° helicopter camera angle (N·V ≈ 0.80), the Fresnel exponent (1 - N·V)^3.6 collapsed to 0.003 (<9% reflectivity). We moved the water pass to run after the full 3D HDR scene resolve (hdr_tex + hdr_depth), depressed 2048×2048 Euclidean SDF pond basins (collar_m ≥ 6.5 m) so straddling terrain triangles never poke through ponds, confined the golden-amber Beer-Lambert littoral shelf to the outer 0..2.2 m shoreline rim, and implemented screen-space Snell refraction plus 32-step 3D ray-marched Screen-Space Reflections (SSR) smoothed with a 9-tap 2D anisotropic water-ripple optical filter.
  2. Replacing the Flat 2D Road Texture with 480,000 True 3D Polyhedral Gravel Stones: In low grazing lead-car shots (cam_car_lead, 0.8 m above the road), 2D Parallax Occlusion Mapping (POM) sheared procedural Voronoi cells (facetedStoneField) into flat, stretched cobblestone patches, while inRut culled 54% of 3D pebbles in the tire tracks. We stripped out the 2D Voronoi/POM illusion, increased lateral road mesh tessellation from 97 to 161 vertices (~6 cm spacing), and populated the entire roadbed with 480,000 hardware-instanced 3D polyhedral fractured gravel stones scaled down 8× to realistic 0.7–3.0 cm crushed aggregate across all four lateral zones.
  3. From Sparse Triangle Blades to a 4.8-Million-Strand 3D Fur Grass Renderer: Spreading 720,000 wide ribbons across a 220 m × 184 m window (40,480 m²) yielded only ~18 strands/m² overall (~78 strands/m² in the verge), making close-up grass look like sparse 4–8 cm wide shark-tooth triangles over bare green ground. We re-engineered the groom into 480,000 hardware-instanced micro-clusters × 10 ultra-fine curved 3D Bezier strands = 4,800,000 individual 3D grass strands (2.6–5.2 mm blade width, >2,600 strands/m² in the foreground camera corridor) with deep root-bed ambient occlusion.

Act IV · Chapter 08 · Horizon Scale, Volumetric Skies & Optical Lens Simulation

1,750 m maximum offline draw distance, horizon cradling, 32-step self-shadowed cumulus clouds, and 12-tap telephoto DoF.

When we inspected high-altitude helicopter captures across the four stages, we asked four pointed questions: Why does the distant ground texture look low-res? Why is there no 3D grass from helicopter height? Why are there tree shadows on the bottom-right with no trees? And why is there an ugly gap between the drawn geometry and the horizon on hilltop stages?

Every single symptom traced back to aggressive real-time chase-camera cutoffs that were never meant for offline cinema rendering—plus un-filtered sub-pixel variance at long range. We overhauled the far-field and post-processing pipeline for maximum offline fidelity:

  • Maximum Offline Draw Distances & Shadow Blocker Validation: Extended procedural terrain micro-bump relief from 34 m to 420 m (and detail textures to 1,800 m); raised the 26 m camera-altitude grass cutoff to 420 m; extended 3D tree draw distance from 440 m to 1,750 m while retaining dense LOD1 foliage out to 520 m (instead of LOD2’s 90% leaf decimation); and added blocker ray-distance validation (proj.z - dCenter) in calcShadow so off-screen hilltop trees behind the camera frustum never project phantom shadows through un-rendered hills.
  • Far-Field DEM Horizon Cradling (>280 m): On hilltop stages like Ruuhimäki, 1.8× vertical exaggeration dropped the outer DEM perimeter 90 m below the stage, creating an exposed valley wedge against the sky. Cradling far-field DEM elevations beyond 280 m and wrapping the perimeter in a continuous >340 m boreal forest belt sealed the horizon completely.
  • 12-Tap Golden-Angle Vogel Oval-Aperture Lens DoF & Nyquist Band-Limiting: To eliminate distant pixelation and reflection shimmer without blurring the hero car, we added a depth-aware 12-tap Golden-Angle Vogel oval-aperture telephoto lens filter (with bilateral depth guards around the focal plane) and pixel-footprint Nyquist bump band-limiting.
  • 32-Step Volumetric Self-Shadowed Cumulus Skies & Soft Canopy Penumbras: Replaced stretched “paint-streak” sky clouds with a 32-step 3D Perlin-Worley cumulus raymarcher featuring 4-step Beer-Lambert solar self-shadowing (tauSun), sunlit silver-lined crowns, and shaded cloud bases—paired with 12-tap Vogel soft woodland canopy penumbra shadows on roadside grass.
