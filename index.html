01 / From a spark to a digital twin

How a conversational prototype became a full rally simulation.

We did not start with a game engine, a pre-built rally track, or a commercial asset pack. We started with raw 10 Hz World Rally Championship telemetry for #69 Kalle Rovanperä and #33 Elfyn Evans, a browser window, and a question: could we conversationally build (“vibe-code”) an interactive 3D WRC visualizer from scratch?

Each time the prototype worked, the next visual or physical limitation became immediately obvious in the frame. Fixing those bottlenecks one by one forced us to outgrow the browser, bring up bare-metal cloud GPUs (AMD Radeon Pro V710 → NVIDIA A100 → NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell), write custom shaders for 260,000 hair-ribbon grass strands and two-phase hydrodynamic spray, subdivide the car to 682,182 triangles, replace synthetic sine-wave scenery with national LiDAR/topographic surveys, and solve 80 Hz nonlinear vehicle dynamics.

1. Multi-source open data fusion

Synchronized 10 Hz WRC telemetry and an A100 CUDA friction-circle optimal ghost (#00) with 30 Hz University of Tartu RTK-GNSS trajectories, Estonian Maa-amet 1 m numeric LiDAR DTMs/nDSMs, Riegl point clouds, ETAK topographic vectors, and Finnish SYKE / Metsäkeskus forest inventories across SS2/SS5 Elva, SS3/SS6 Neeruti, SS4/SS7 Otepää and SS22 Ruuhimäki.

2. Custom cloud GPU rendering pipeline

When browser WebGL hit its ceiling, we moved rendering to cloud GPUs streaming 1080p frames back to the browser HUD. The renderer combines a 260,000-strand Cubic-Bezier hair grass system, an 8-species 4.10M-triangle scanned botanical forest, crown-first 3D Perlin-Worley cumulus clouds, two-phase gravel dust and Weber-breakup puddle spray, and corridor-relative golden-hour lighting.

3. 682k-triangle car & 80 Hz 6-DOF physics

The monolithic car GLB was topologically split into five articulated assemblies (body + FL/FR/RL/RR), subdivided to 682,182 curved PN-triangles with native 8K PBR livery and 12-pass 3D rotational wheel blur, and driven by an 80 Hz sub-stepped single-track bicycle ODE fusing onboard accx/accy G-sensors, a +0.78 m front-axle drift pivot, parabolic crest flight, and decoupled unsprung wheel knuckles.

4. Survey-grounded world & honest audit

Every major visual claim on this page is backed by matched before/after captures, 1080p/30 FPS motion clips, and explicit root-cause diagnostics—including the dead-ends we had to throw away (“bare brown sticks” tree imports, crushed ink-black shadows, faceted far-canopy sheets, and weightless turntable yaw filters).

“we have this VM, can we do remote rendering from there - vmCopilotV710”

Before any cloud GPU servers, scanned botanical trees, or hair-ribbon grass existed, the very first version of TGR-WRT-demo ran entirely in the browser as a client-side Three.js / WebGL application (src/main.js and src/carMesh.js). Even at this earliest stage, the core analytical idea was already in place: synchronizing #69 Kalle Rovanperä, #33 Elfyn Evans, and the #00 A100 CUDA Optimal friction-circle ghost across SS22 Ruuhimäki, SS13 Neeruti, SS14 Elva and SS12 Otepää with live speed/throttle/brake telemetry traces and a 1 m LiDAR elevation profile scrubber.

Visually, however, the browser WebGL scene was purely schematic: the cars were blocky procedural extrusions with glowing orange/red proximity rings, the stage road was a flat pink-and-mint ribbon, LiDAR returns were floating colored dots, and the terrain was a low-resolution vertex-colored green heightmap with zero trees or grass. Chapter 01Commits 98e9116 → 609a8f1AMD Radeon Pro V710 MxGPU (54 RDNA 3 Ray Accelerators)

Origin: Fusing Five Disconnected Open Datasets & First Cloud Hardware Ray-Tracing Bring-Up

The project began from a fundamental data gap: high-resolution 3D terrain scans and competitive WRC rally telemetry are never published together. To build a physically grounded 3D Digital Twin for Toyota GAZOO Racing (TGR-WRT) without proprietary stage packs, we engineered an automated data-fusion pipeline combining five open scientific and government sources:

  • WRC 10 Hz Merged Telemetry & CUDA Optimal Ghost (#69 Rovanperä, #33 Evans, #00 Optimal): Synchronized speed, RPM, gear, throttle, brake, steering and 3D GPS traces across SS2/SS5 Elva, SS3/SS6 Neeruti, SS4/SS7 Otepää and Ruuhimäki, paired with an offline GPU forward-backward friction-circle optimal-control solver (#00).
  • University of Tartu Autonomous Driving Lab (UT-ADL) 30 Hz RTK-GNSS: Extracted厘米-accurate trajectories from summer2021-positions.parquet after discovering the exact local-to-L-EST97 coordinate offset (E = 650000 + x, N = 6465000 + y in EPSG:3301).
  • Estonian Land Board (Maa-amet) 1 m Float32 LiDAR DTM & Riegl VQ-1560i LAZ Point Clouds: Parsed raw 1 m elevation rasters and 2023 airborne laser scanning returns (ground class 2, water class 9, buildings class 6, vegetation classes 3/4/5) alongside 2048×2048 WMS aerial orthophotos.

Because the local workstation environment exposed only a virtual Microsoft Hyper-V Video adapter, we provisioned cloud GPU instances (vmCopilotA100 and vmCopilotV710). On the AMD Radeon Pro V710, the Linux kernel initially failed to initialize the GPU with missing firmware amdgpu/gc_11_0_3_mes1.bin (error -2). Injecting the gc_11_0_3 firmware suite into initramfs unlocked all 54 RDNA 3 hardware Ray Accelerators (VK_KHR_acceleration_structure + VK_KHR_ray_query), powering a custom C++/Vulkan ray-query bridge (libv710_vk_rt.so) over a 36,000-triangle terrain/car BLAS. Visually, however, the first live frames were purely geometric: trees were procedural green cones and the road was a simple ribbon.

Chapter 02Commits 55f525d, c02c88e, a335de7260,000 Strands / 1.82M Vertices + 54k 3D Pebbles

The Grass & Micro-Surface Revolution: 260,000 Cubic-Bezier Hair Strands & 32-Bit Spline Interpolation

“I don't like the grass, this is just tufts here and there. We should have something like hair systems… Which rendering engine are we using actually? Should we switch to unreal or unity?”

Rather than scattering isolated cross-quad grass tufts (`v710_chase_moss_v1.jpg`), we architected a UE5-Groom / Ghost of Tsushima style instanced 3D curved Cubic-Bezier hair-ribbon grass system (while also generating a companion UE5.4 C++/Python project scaffold with Niagara dust and Chaos Vehicle configs):

  • 260,000 Dynamic 7-Vertex Curved Ribbons (1.82M vertices): Continuously recycled inside a camera-locked 92 m rolling field around the car, with species zones (tall golden-headed Calamagrostis reed grass on outer banks, lush Festuca meadow fescue on ditch slopes, and short Poa pratensis verge turf near the gravel edge).
  • Physical Fiber Shading: Combined Kajiya-Kay anisotropic longitudinal strand highlights with Marschner backlit subsurface chlorophyll transmission and a 6-layer volumetric moss shell raymarcher on ditch shoulders.
  • The 5.5 m Texture-Filter Snapping Bug: Early hair-grass renders exhibited periodic step-snapping along the road every 5.5 m. Root cause: ModernGL mapped a 2-tuple filter on a 32-bit float road-spline texture to (LINEAR, NEAREST), disabling hardware linear interpolation! Switching to explicit sub-texel texelFetch linear interpolation in GLSL eliminated the stepping completely. We also added 54,000 instanced 3D gravel pebbles in a 28 m rolling window.
Chapter 03Commits ab66786, bbb12a9Native 1080p + Shadow FBO Fix + Procedural Tree Pivot

Ground-Truth Rally Estonia Calibration, Depth-Buffer Shadow Fix & The Procedural Tree Dead-End

“ok stop, this is a dead end. do extensive research first to see best practices in generating procedural trees, failing that, just download very high poly tree models that fit… abandon these generated trees, they look terrible.”

With the grass carpet working, we audited the renderer against eight real WRC Rally Estonia photographs from Otepää, Elva and Tartu, upgraded the live stream pipeline from 720p to native 1920×1080 (bbb12a9), and uncovered two critical rendering bugs:

  • Missing Real 3D Shadows after Frame 0: Calling glDisable(GL_DEPTH_TEST) at the end of render_frame left depth testing disabled when the next frame's shadow_fbo pass began. Because OpenGL suppresses depth-buffer writes whenever GL_DEPTH_TEST is disabled, the 2048×2048 shadow map stayed frozen at 1.0 after frame 0, leaving only a rectangular analytic traceCarBox proxy under the car!
  • Double-Attenuated Ackermann Steering (0.075 × 0.075): Steering wheel angle was multiplied by 0.075 both during telemetry ingestion and inside render_frame, reducing a 25° wheel lock to an imperceptible 0.3°.
  • The Procedural Tree Card-Atlas Dead-End: We built a 5-column procedural tree atlas (baltic_3d_tree_atlas_v6) with normal-mapped bark and foliage cards (`v710_v6_low_orbit_trees.jpg`). Under close orbit inspection, intersecting alpha cards still read as flat paper cutouts. Following the user's pivot, we abandoned procedural card trees entirely in favor of real 3D botanical geometry.
Chapter 04Commit 2ff3a0d4.10M Triangles / 8 Species × 3 LODs

From “Bare Brown Sticks” to the 4.10M-Triangle 8-Species Botanical Bundle

When we first imported raw 3D tree GLTF models from Poly Haven (`v710_ph3d_chase.jpg` and `v710_ph3d_forest_orbit.jpg`), the result was a visual shock: the trees rendered as dead, skeletal brown sticks. A four-part forensic diagnosis revealed why naive 3D plant imports fail at rally viewing distances:

  1. 97.5% Canopy Surface-Area Loss from Uncompensated Subsampling: The source conifer scans modeled 4.14M individual 1 cm × 2 mm 3D needles (148 m² total needle area). Subsampling to 100k triangles without scaling up the surviving needle clusters reduced canopy area to 3.6 m²!
  2. 75% Alpha-Card Knockout & Black Mip-Bleeding on 3D Needles: A twig-card alpha texture (75% transparent [0,0,0,0] pixels) was bound to already-3D-modeled individual needles and leaves. Worse, trilinear mipmapping averaged the transparent [0,0,0,0] background into the needle RGB texels (`mean_rgb = [27, 27, 15]`), turning surviving foliage pitch-black at distance.
  3. Dry South African Scan Albedo: The raw scans had drought-brown foliage (R > G, [101, 98, 46]) instead of lush July Estonian chlorophyll (G/R = 1.30).
  4. Missing Lower Canopy Skirts: High-branching scans had no foliage below y = 0.45, leaving an empty trunk hallway at the 2.8 m chase-camera eye height.

We built scripts/compile_ultra_highpoly_forest.py to compile 13 Poly Haven botanical scans into an 8-species × 3-LOD runtime bundle (4.10M LOD0 triangles, 2,068,548 vertices): finding exact topological needle/leaf clusters via scipy.sparse.csgraph.connected_components and scaling each cluster around its 3D centroid, forcing alpha = 255 with 5-pass morphological solid-color dilation, re-grading foliage to Estonian summer chlorophyll, and grafting lower canopy skirts (`uhp` v1 → v3 → v7).

Breaking the “Procedural Corridor”: Survey-Grounded Landscapes, Metsaserv Edges & Temporal Stability

Even with 4.10M-triangle scanned trees, 260k-strand grass, a 682k-triangle car and golden-hour lighting, all four rally stages still looked like the same uniform green tunnel. Our final investigation — documented in full detail across Sections 03 through 10 of this report — traced that homogeneity to six structural root causes in _build_forest_mesh and the vertex shaders:

  1. Unused Real Geodata: _build_forest_mesh ignored LiDAR canopy heights and orthophotos, driving all four stages with the same 1D trigonometric wave _eval_biome_openness(s_m, side).
  2. Savanna Thinning vs. Binary Stand Mosaic: Dropping trees via independent Bernoulli trials (prob = base_prob * (1 - openness * 0.85)) turned closed forests into sparse golf-course savannas instead of alternating between 100% open agricultural meadows and tight, closed-canopy stands.
  3. The w_s Crown-Width Illusion: Height-normalized conifer meshes have a model-space radius of only 0.13..0.25 units, making their true world-space crowns 4× narrower than nominal w_s assumed.
  4. Missing 3-Tier Estonian Metsaserv (Forest Edge): Real hemiboreal stand boundaries seal the lower 0–10 m with a continuous curtain of ditch ferns/shrubs (skirt), grey alder/hazel/willow saplings (mantle), and interlocking pine/spruce/birch crowns (overstory).
  5. Post-Slice Frustum Culling & Distant Component Thinning: Slicing [:n2] before frustum culling let off-screen trees consume half the visible quota, while fol_all[::2] / fol_all[::4] thinned distant crowns.
  6. The 345 m Connected-Mesh Clip-Space Teleportation Bug: Moving individual vertices beyond 345 m to gl_Position = vec4(2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 1.0) stretched boundary triangles across the screen and caused paused-frame flicker (max diff 105 → 1 after restoring hardware frustum clipping and 1.0 far sky depth).

Giving the Car Real Mass: Telemetry-Coupled 80 Hz Vehicle Dynamics, Front-Axle Drift Pivoting & Unsprung Wheel Articulation

“the cars move in a way that doesn’t quite seem believable, they don’t seem to have the ‘weight’ behind it. Especially the heading, it seems the cars just turn on a dime, as if the steering wheel directly changes the heading (as if it’s on a platform).”

Inspecting the car’s motion in playback exposed five mathematical causes behind that “weightless / turntable” feel: (1) lateral offset (lat_offset) and yaw slip (slip_angle) were solved as two uncoupled filters while heading referenced the static road centerline tangent rather than the car’s displaced velocity vector (“crabbing on a dolly”); (2) corner-entry yaw added up to 23° of algebraic curvature slip around the center (0,0,0) while rut_yaw_kicks injected a synthetic 2.0 Hz straight-line yaw sine wiggle (±6.5° to ±22.9° on straightaways); (3) real 10 Hz onboard accelerometer channels (accx, accy) in the WRC telemetry JSON were ignored (and had inverted axis polarity on ruuhimaki); (4) all four wheels were multiplied by the sprung chassis_mat (w_world = chassis_mat @ w_local), tilting the tires rigidly off the road when the body rolled or dived; and (5) crest jumps used a clamped symmetric linear spring (ζ = 0.28) with no parabolic free-fall or rebound damping control.

In commit dd02879 (scripts/v710_renderer.py, scripts/a100_renderer.py, src/main.js, src/carMesh.js), we replaced the uncoupled filters with a coupled 80 Hz sub-stepped nonlinear 6-DOF rally physics engine. 12-Pass 3D Rotational Wheel Blur, Tire Contact Squish & Enclosed Kevlar Wheel Liners

In earlier builds, high-speed motion blur was approximated in the post-process shader using a hardcoded 120 px screen-space UV ellipse (carEllip, RCA-1), while the 3D wheel geometry itself was drawn in a single static pass (0 rotational shutter samples). At 142 km/h on SS2/SS5 Elva, this produced two simultaneous visual contradictions:

  • Smudged Bodywork + Frozen Spokes: The screen-space UV ellipse smeared the stationary Panasonic rear wing, #69 door number, and TOYOTA tailgate horizontally across 120 pixels, yet the white 12-spoke OZ Racing gravel rims remained completely frozen as if parked.
  • Hollow Wheel-Arch Sky Leaks (RCA-4) & Rigid Tires (RCA-3): Because the raw car shell lacked inner fender wells, bright sky and background grass leaked straight through the gap behind the front tires, and the tires floated rigidly without radial load deflection at the gravel contact patch.

The Fix: We removed carEllip screen-space smearing from the car body and implemented true 12-pass 3D rotational shutter accumulation (N_WHEEL_BLUR = 12 sub-frame rotor steps distributed across a 180° shutter angle Δθ = ω · Δtshutter around each wheel’s 3D hub axis), added radial contact-patch sidewall bulge (RCA-3), and sealed all four wheel wells with 3D matte Kevlar inner fender liners (RCA-4).

Mesh & Material Upgrade: 231k Faceted & 50%-Decimated Rivals → 682,182 Curved PN-Triangles & Native 8K PBR

Close-up cameras exposed two separate geometric bottlenecks — one on the hero car and a catastrophic decimation bug on the pack rival cars:

  • Hero Mesh Subdivision & Native 8K PBR Pipeline (5b03395): The original 231,098-triangle GLB exhibited polygonal, faceted silhouettes along the flared carbon wheel arches and front splitter, while its diffuse texture had been downscaled to 4096×4096, softening thin sponsor scripts (DMG MORI, CCI, DENSO, Pirelli). Articulated 5-Part Wheels: Topological Mesh Separation, Ackermann Steering & Braking Dive Kinematics

The raw Toyota GR Yaris Rally1 asset was delivered as a single monolithic static mesh — the wheels, tires, brake calipers, coilover struts, and chassis were all fused into one vertex buffer. To make the car respond physically to 10 Hz / 30 Hz WRC telemetry, we engineered a three-stage mechanical articulation pipeline:

  1. 5-Part Topological Connected-Component Separation: Using scipy.sparse.csgraph.connected_components combined with axle spatial bounding volumes (z_front = +1.23 m, z_rear = -1.32 m, y_axle = 0.33 m, r_wheel = 0.35 m), we partitioned the mesh into five independent articulated groups (body + FL, FR, RL, RR wheel/tire/rim assemblies) while keeping the upper orange/gold coilover suspension struts rigidly attached to the body shell so they never spin with the wheel (`yaris_5part_topological_split.jpg`).
  2. True Ackermann Kingpin Steering & Cumulative Axle Spin (ab66786): Early builds multiplied steering wheel telemetry by 0.075 during ingestion and again by 0.075 inside render_frame (0.075 × 0.075 = 0.005625), clamping a 25° cornering lock down to an invisible 0.3°! Fixing the double attenuation restored full ±25° kingpin yaw articulation (Y axis) composed with cumulative angular wheel integration θ(t) = ∫(v / rtire) dt around each hub’s local X axis (`zoom_articulated_wheels_macro.jpg`).
  3. Dynamic Braking Pitch, 880 °C Glowing Brake Rotors & Steered Rotor Blur (69d7330, 13cfd20): Under heavy braking entering the chicane, longitudinal deceleration drives forward chassis pitch/dive, compresses the front wheel arch down over the tire, heats the ventilated steel brake discs to a visible 880 °C orange-red thermal glow behind the calipers, and composes kingpin steering lock with 12-pass 3D rotational spoke blur (`zoom_braking_dive_macro.jpg`).

03 / Grounding & corrections

Use the actual products, not their preview colours.

The most consequential correction to the proposal was to stop treating *_ndsm.jpg as a calibrated metre-scale height raster. A plausible grayscale or coloured height preview does not establish its scale, no-data convention, legend, acquisition date or accuracy. The implementation downloads and reads the numeric Estonian GeoTIFFs, and explicitly treats Finland's source as classified height bands.

04 / Engineering

One world field, shared by the scene.

01 / INPUTS

Numeric terrain and canopy; semantic vectors; Finnish height classes and inventory; existing route and imagery.

02 / WORLD FIELD

Stand footprint, height prior, validity, metric edge distance, land cover, moisture/tint and provenance.

03 / SCENE

Overstory, edge mantle, regeneration, ground/grass, mapped foundations and water, contextual road furniture.

04 / PRESERVATION

Spatial indexing, conservative bounds, stable LODs, cutout filtering, shared wind, planned cameras and measured output.

Area-based woodland and edge structure

Overstory candidates use a 3.8 m world-cell lattice with deterministic jitter and bounded density priors. A separate 2.8 m edge layer supplies shrubs, saplings and small broadleaves; a 7.5 m interior layer introduces sparse regeneration. These spacings are implementation choices, not claims that every Estonian or Finnish forest has one universal stem density. Composite sapling and shrub assets also mean an instance count is not a stem count.

Canopy height and stand boundaries determine eligibility. Water, buildings, rock, roads and the engineered road clearance exclude unsuitable placements. Source-supported isolated patches can survive in open land. The model uses a jittered area process, not a fully calibrated Thomas-process, crown-competition or silvicultural growth simulation.

Measured crown scale rather than nominal width

Meshes are normalized by height, so horizontal instance scale is not a crown diameter. The renderer measures the loaded geometry and derives scale from a desired crown span and actual model radius. Rendering that retains the intended woodland

Per-species spatial indexes restrict the query region, then conservative union bounds are frustum-culled before LOD selection. There is no nearest-N species quota silently reshuffling visible trees. Projected-size selection uses hysteresis; discontinuous seeks/cuts reset it, while a stationary pause does not force a new geometry choice. Complete authored LOD2 foliage is retained at distance, avoiding the previous derived half/quarter-component thinning.

Coverage factors are measured per texture layer and mip level, with bounded scale and an anisotropy-aware lookup. They support alpha-to-coverage and 8x MSAA; they are not an exact optical-depth invariant. Colour, shadow and velocity passes share deformation and cutout logic. Fine ground noise is attenuated when undersampled, and foliage chromatic variation no longer slides through a high-frequency world-space sine as the leaves move.

Detailed crowns fade over 340–430 m into mapped ground treatment and haze. An elevated, source-shaped canopy-sheet prototype was tested and rejected: it produced faceted, blanket-like horizons in wide views. It is not shipped or counted as a visual improvement. Proper distant volumetric/impostor crowns remain future asset work.

A separate wide-view defect came from an existing vertex-culling shortcut: connected terrain and road vertices beyond 345 m were moved individually to one clip-space point without initializing their varyings; the scenery shader had the same defect at 180 m. Mixed triangles stretched across the image. Ordinary GPU primitive clipping now preserves the surface, and the sky fills only untouched far depth. An actual GPU regression checks surface/sky ordering at 3 km.

Terrain, ground and landmarks

The broad road-height floor is removed. A local road/ditch correction ends at 16 m, with modest procedural surface displacement and separate mapped foundation/water adjustments. Vertical presentation exaggeration remains explicit. Estonia uses a denser derivative from the numeric DTM; Finland retains the existing terrain source with denser interpolation, not a newly surveyed 1 m Finnish surface.

Terrain and grass read the same woodland/moisture/tint masks. Open agricultural ground, woodland litter and moist edges therefore change together. Mapped buildings receive contained generic proxies, not automatically generated silos, tractors and companion buildings. Water follows mapped polygon boundaries; estimated levels and approximate reflections are qualified below. Signs and anti-cut bales are sparse curvature-derived illustrations, not claimed observations of the historic course.

Road wetness is derived from smoothed vertical sags and canopy shade. The GPU road surface and CPU tire emissions use the same profile. This removes the stage-independent periodic basin field without pretending to know the actual rally-day rainfall or drainage.

Camera clearance is planned, not hidden

Automatic rigs smooth the horizontal reference with acceleration/jerk checks, anticipate conservative crown and static-scenery bounds, and construct a vertically speed-bounded clearance majorant. The resulting C2 path is independent of render wall time and frame partition. A complete-route 120 Hz audit samples terrain, wind-inflated crown cylinders and building/scenery bounds before acceptance. The old independent gimbal is replaced by subject-directed aiming; manual bumper/orbit controls remain.

Conservative full crown cylinders can unnecessarily lift the camera above empty space beneath high branches. That is a deliberate safety/appearance trade-off, visible in the separately labelled native clips. It is not a proof of triangle-level collision freedom or a complete robotics planner.

08 / Negative evidence & boundaries

What was rejected, qualified or left approximate.

Rejected pilot: correct trees, unsafe old camera

The first geographic candidate still put an old aerial eye inside a crown. It was not accepted as a finished improvement, and trees were not deleted to clear the lens. The new path planner addresses that presentation failure.

Revised camera: a different view, explicitly labelled

The second pilot demonstrates clearance at the same route time, but changes the eye and framing. It must not be used as evidence that a shader alone fixed the view.

Rejected far-canopy prototype and a real pause-stability defect

The first large candidate corpus passed media-format checks but failed visual review. Wide landmark shots exposed a faceted elevated canopy sheet and missing/stretched ground at the old 345 m vertex boundary. That corpus was retained, not promoted. Removing only a fragment cutoff and moving the sky to true far depth was insufficient. Fixing connected-mesh clipping removed the paused-frame instability; a separate on/off ablation then showed that the canopy sheet still looked worse than the underlying mapped terrain, so it was removed rather than disguised.

In the controlled Elva fixed-time probe, the rejected version differed by up to 105 channel levels, with 0.0702% of pixels exceeding an 8-level maximum-channel difference. After the clipping fix, eight repetitions in each of five pass ablations had maximum difference 1/255 and no pixels above that diagnostic threshold. Particle-state hashes and LOD counts stayed fixed in both versions. The final main-corpus repeated-render numbers are reported separately above; this is not a universal motion-flicker score.
